How to create an invoice

An invoice is a dated request for payment. It names who is billing, who is being billed, what was delivered, and when money is due. This page shows a workflow you can follow in InvoiceCraft Pro or on paper.

1. Identify the seller

Use the legal name clients already know from your contract or email signature. Add a street address if your region requires it on tax invoices. Add email and phone so the client can ask about a line item without hunting for you.

If you charge VAT or sales tax, put your registration number in the Tax ID field. Missing IDs are a common reason finance teams bounce invoices.

2. Identify the buyer

Match the client’s legal entity, not a nickname. If they gave you a purchase order number, put it in the description of the first line or in the notes. Large companies often cannot pay an invoice that does not match their vendor record.

3. Number and date the document

Pick a unique invoice number and never reuse it. A simple pattern is INV-YEAR-001. Set the invoice date to the day you issue it. Set a due date you stated in the contract — for example net 15 or net 30. The generator lets you type both dates.

4. Describe the work

Each row should answer: what, how many, and at what rate. “Website project” is weaker than “Landing page design — 12 hours at 85”. Vague lines delay payment because the client cannot map them to a statement of work.

5. Add tax, discount, and extras only if they apply

Enter a tax percentage only when you are required to collect it. Discounts should match a written offer. Shipping or extra fees belong on their own line so the client sees why the total moved.

6. Say how to pay

Use the payment box for bank transfer details, PayPal, Wise, or UPI — whichever you actually accept. Do not paste full card numbers. State late-fee policy only if it is in your agreement.

7. Preview, then export

Check spelling, currency symbol, and the status badge (Unpaid vs Paid). Download PDF or print. Keep a copy. If you use the Save button, remember that draft lives in this browser only.

After you send it

Send the PDF from a mailbox the client recognizes. If payment is late, resend the same invoice number rather than creating a duplicate. When paid, you can mark the status Paid in a new export for your records.
